# Judgment Systems

English | [简体中文](./judgment-systems.zh.md)

## Why Stronger AI Makes Explicit Judgment More Important

A common first question is: *“If AI models keep getting smarter, won't KDNA become unnecessary?”*

In practice, the opposite happens. Weak models fail because they lack basic capability. **Strong models fail because they can generate five plausible, eloquent directions at once—and have no way to know which standard, trade-off priority, or taste belongs to you.**

A brand advisor looks for trust signals. A growth lead optimizes conversion. A minimalist editor cuts every redundant phrase. A conservative engineer guards data integrity above delivery speed. None of them are “wrong”—they simply operate from different judgment systems.

The real question in AI collaboration is no longer *“Can the model reason?”* It is:

**Whose judgment should guide this work, under what conditions, and where must it stop?**

---

## 1. Judgment vs. Information

Give the exact same 500-page book to a beginner and a seasoned practitioner. They hold identical information. The difference is **attention and priority**: the practitioner knows which three questions decide the outcome, which tempting shortcuts backfire, and when a rule stops applying.

Documents and knowledge bases store facts and explanations. A **judgment system** captures how a person or team weighs those facts:

- What core variable you watch first
- What wins when two good goals collide
- What counts as good taste and restraint
- What signals mean “stop and hand this back to a human”
- How common misuses happen even when people repeat the right slogans

---

## 2. From One Judgment to a Connected System

A single `.kdna` work can hold a handful of sharp judgments on one focused craft or dozens of interconnected judgments across a full domain. What holds a judgment system together is structure:

- **Shared kernel**: the overarching question, core worldview, and value order behind the work.
- **Individual judgments**: each anchored to a concrete question (`focus`), with clear claims, reasons, methods, examples, boundaries, exceptions, and anti-misuse warnings.
- **Explicit relationships**: `support` (supporting premises), `qualify` (limiting conditions), `conflict` (real tensions that must be weighed together), and `complement` (related perspectives).

When an AI Agent selects one judgment via `exact_selection`, KDNA delivers its upstream supporting premises, qualifying limits, and conflicting tensions together—preventing the model from quoting a conclusion out of context.

---

## 3. Not a Digital Clone

KDNA does not try to copy an entire mind or turn a person into a file. One person naturally holds different standards for different roles—you can maintain separate `.kdna` works for writing, design review, hiring, or architecture, share some publicly, keep others encrypted locally, and revise each one independently as your thinking matures.

---

## 4. Authorship & Accountability

`.kdna` works can be authored by individuals, teams, or created in dialogue with AI tools. Whatever workflow produces a work, its manifest and attributions record clearly who created, reviewed, or confirmed it—so readers and collaborators always know whose standard they are looking at before choosing to adopt it.

**In one sentence:** KDNA does not exist because AI models are weak. It exists because even the strongest AI needs an open, portable, human-readable way to work by a specific person's or team's judgment.
